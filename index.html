Defining pressure

A pressure ball is one in the top 25% of the Pressure Index.

PRESSURE INDEX (per ball)
PI = (0.5 × LI + 0.5 × SSI) × M

LI · Leverage: expected swing in win probability from the next ball, averaged over realistic outcomes (dot, 1, 2, 4, 6, wicket). Win-probability models are gradient-boosted classifiers with monotonic constraints, cross-validated by match.

SSI · Scoreboard stress: equal mix of stage of innings, required-rate squeeze, collapse in progress (wickets in last 18 balls) and closeness of contest.

M · Stakes: ×1.25 in knockouts and finals.

A pressure ball = top 25% of PI (PI ≥ ).

Why blend? Leverage alone over-flags calm early overs where the game is still 50:50; stress alone ignores what is at stake. Together they find the balls where the game, and the player, are both on edge.

How the Clutch Score is built

35% Pressure impact – win-probability added per 100 pressure balls. 25% Runs above expected – vs league average for the same innings, phase, wickets lost and format; a wicket costs 8 runs. 15% Lift – pressure output minus own non-pressure output. 15% Consistency – share of innings/spells (6+ pressure balls) with positive impact, shrunk to 50% over 3 games. 10% Volume – log of pressure balls.

Eligibility: 150+ legal pressure balls and played in 2026. Rates shrunk toward the pool mean by 60 balls. Recency weights 2022→2026: 0.6, 0.7, 0.8, 0.9, 1.0. Games involving associates or A-teams weighted 0.6. Each component z-scored, weighted and summed.

Limitations
  • First-innings win probability is soft without venue/pitch data (AUC 0.69 vs 0.90 when chasing).
  • Batting position shapes which pressure a player can face; a role-adjusted baseline is next.
  • Fielding and captaincy are not in ball-by-ball data.
  • T20I opposition strength is only coarsely adjusted (associates weighted 0.6).
  • Batter-hand vs bowler-type matchups are not yet used.
